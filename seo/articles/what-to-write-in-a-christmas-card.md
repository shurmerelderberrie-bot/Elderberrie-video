There's a particular kind of December evening where the cards are bought, the pen is out, and your mind goes completely blank. You've written "Merry Christmas, love from us" eleven times and it's starting to feel like a form you're filling in.

The good news is that a Christmas card doesn't need to be clever. People keep the ones that sound like you. Here are 80 messages to borrow, tweak or use as a starting point, sorted by who you're writing to.

## How to make any Christmas message feel personal

- **Use their name.** "Dear Sam" reads warmer than no greeting at all.
- **Add one specific line.** A memory from this year, something you're looking forward to, or a nod to their news ("Congratulations on the new house!").
- **Look ahead.** A hope for the new year turns a greeting into a conversation.
- **Keep it short if you're stuck.** Two honest sentences beat a paragraph you don't mean.

## Questions people ask

### What should I write in a Christmas card if I don't know someone well?

Keep it simple and warm: "Wishing you a merry Christmas and a happy new year" plus their name and yours is perfectly polite. If you want to add one line, thank them for something specific, such as help at work or taking in a parcel.

### Is it OK to write "Season's greetings" instead of "Merry Christmas"?

Yes. "Season's greetings" or "Happy holidays" are good choices for colleagues, clients, or anyone you're not sure celebrates Christmas.

### When should I send Christmas cards?

Most people send them in early December. If you're posting in the UK this year, Royal Mail's last recommended dates are Friday 18 December for 2nd Class and Monday 21 December for 1st Class. After that, a printable card you hand over yourself is the easiest option.
