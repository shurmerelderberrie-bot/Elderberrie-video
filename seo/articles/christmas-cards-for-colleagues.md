Office Christmas cards are a funny thing. Nobody wants to be the person who forgot, but writing thirty cards for people you mostly know from the kettle queue can feel like a lot.

This guide gives you messages for every kind of work relationship, from your closest desk buddy to the boss, plus a simple way to do cards for the whole team without spending a fortune.

## Do you have to give Christmas cards to colleagues?

No, it's entirely up to you, and plenty of people don't. If you do, there are a few easy ways to keep it fair and stress-free:

- **One card for the team.** A single card on the shared desk or in the staff room, signed "Merry Christmas everyone".
- **A card for everyone you work closely with.** Your own team, plus anyone who helped you out this year.
- **The same design for all.** It saves time and nobody wonders why theirs was different.

If your workplace is mixed, "Season's greetings" or "Happy holidays" work for everyone, whatever they celebrate.

## Questions people ask

### Can I write the same message in every colleague's card?

Yes. A short, warm message such as "Wishing you a restful Christmas and a happy new year" works for everyone. If you have time, add a name and one personal line to the cards for the people you work with most.

### Should I give my boss a Christmas card?

It's a nice gesture but not expected. Keep it simple and appreciative, for example "Thank you for your support this year. Have a lovely Christmas."

### Can I print one printable card for my whole team?

With our cards, yes. You can print them as many times as you need for family, friends and colleagues. Please just don't resell or share the files.

### What's a good non-religious Christmas card message for work?

"Season's greetings and best wishes for the new year" or "Wishing you a restful break and a happy new year" both work for everyone.
