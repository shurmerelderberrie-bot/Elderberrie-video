The end of the autumn term is when many families say thank you to the people who look after their children all week. A Christmas card is the simplest way to do it, and teachers really do keep them.

You don't need anything elaborate. Here are 45 ideas you can use as they are or adapt, sorted by who's writing and who it's for.

## A few tips first

- **Let your child do some of it.** Even a wobbly name or a drawing means more than perfect handwriting from you.
- **Mention one thing.** "Thank you for helping Ellie with her reading" is the line a teacher remembers.
- **Don't forget the team.** Teaching assistants, nursery key workers, lunchtime staff and the school office do a lot behind the scenes.
- **Keep it short.** Teachers read a lot of cards in the last week of term.

## Questions people ask

### Do you have to give teachers a Christmas card?

No, it's never expected, but most teachers say a card with a few words from your child is the thing they treasure most.

### What do you write in a card for a teacher you don't know well?

A short thank you is perfect: "Thank you for all your hard work this term. Have a lovely Christmas." Add your child's name so they know who it's from.

### Should you give a card to the teaching assistant too?

If you can, yes. Teaching assistants often work closely with children every day, and a card from your child makes their week.

### What can a child who can't write yet put in a teacher's card?

Their name, a drawing, or a handprint. You can write "Merry Christmas, love from" and let them add their name, however wobbly.
