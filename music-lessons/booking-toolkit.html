Email templates

Copy a template into your email, fill in the highlighted parts and send. Bank details only ever go in these emails, never on the public page.

1. Reply to a lesson enquiry

For guitar, singing, drums, mentoring or a taster.

Subject: Re: [their subject]

Hi [Name],

Thanks so much for getting in touch about [guitar lessons]. I'd love to help.

I've got space on [day and time] or [day and time]. Lessons are £20 for 30 minutes or £35 for an hour, and if you'd like to try it first, a 30-minute taster is £10. [For drums: lessons are at your place, on your own kit.]

Let me know what works for you and I'll send over a booking confirmation with all the details.

Thanks,
Joseph

2. Reply to a church enquiry

Suggest a chat first, keep the price open.

Subject: Re: [their subject]

Hi [Name],

Thanks for getting in touch about a session with [Church name]'s worship team. I'd love to come and help.

It would be great to have a quick chat first, by phone, video or over a coffee, so I can hear about your team, what's going well and what you'd like help with. Then we can shape the session around that, whether it's a band practice, a workshop or a jam session.

My usual price is £120 for up to 2 hours, including preparation and travel on the Island. Every church is different, though, so if budget is a concern just say and we'll work something out.

Would [day and time] or [day and time] suit you for a chat?

Thanks,
Joseph

Getting paid

Payment set-up

  • Bank transfer is the simplest way to be paidIt's free, it leaves a record, and people are used to it. Ask for the student's name or the invoice number as the reference so you can match payments.
  • A separate account for lesson moneyIt isn't required, but it makes your tax return much easier.
  • When to take paymentLessons and tasters: before the lesson, or monthly in advance for regular students. Churches: invoice after the session, due within 14 days.
  • Keep a simple recordA spreadsheet with date, student, amount and date paid, plus your costs (fuel, strings, insurance, adverts). That's all you need for Self Assessment.
  • Keep bank details privateThey go in confirmation emails and invoices only, never on the public page or social media.
Doing it properly

Legal checklist

The page and templates already cover the consumer and privacy points. These are the things only you can do.

  • Tell HMRC you're self-employedOnce your lesson income is over £1,000 in a tax year, register for Self Assessment by 5 October after that tax year ends. Keep records for at least 5 years after the 31 January filing deadline. gov.uk/working-for-yourself
  • Always send the booking confirmationPeople who book by email or online have a 14-day right to cancel. If you don't tell them in writing, that period can stretch to 12 months, and they may not have to pay for lessons already given. Templates 3 and 4 do this for you. If someone cancels, refund them within 14 days.
  • Clear, all-in prices and genuine reviewsPrices must include everything (no surprise extras), and reviews must be real and not edited to change their meaning. Both are now enforced under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024. Your page already does this. Ask before using a reviewer's name.
  • Check the ICO data protection feeMost small businesses that hold customer details on a phone or computer pay it. The smallest tier is £52 a year, and some sole traders are exempt. Take the ICO's quick self-assessment to find out. ico.org.uk data protection fee
  • Keep enquiries tidyDelete enquiries that don't turn into bookings after a few months, keep your phone and email locked, and only keep what you need.
  • Your addressThe E-Commerce Regulations ask business websites to show a postal address as well as your name and email. If you'd rather not show your home address, use a business address or PO box, and I'll add it to the page.
  • Safeguarding and DBSFor under-18s, keep contact through parents and have a parent in the house. You can apply for a basic DBS check yourself; an enhanced check usually comes through an organisation such as your church. For church sessions, follow that church's safeguarding policy. gov.uk basic DBS check
  • Public liability insuranceCovers you if someone is hurt or something is damaged during a lesson, especially in people's homes and churches. Musicians' Union membership includes it, or use a music teacher policy.
  • VATNothing to do unless your turnover goes over £90,000 a year.

This is general guidance on UK rules as of late 2026, not legal advice. If anything is unclear for your situation, gov.uk and the ICO have free guidance, and Citizens Advice runs a business advice line.
